# 04 — Feature Guide: Reminders

Reminders are the core of the daily schedule. They ensure the patient knows what to do and when to do it.

---

## 1. Simple vs. Guided Reminders

When setting up a reminder, a caregiver can choose between two types:

### Simple Reminders
These are for quick, one-action tasks. 
*   **Example:** "Drink a glass of water" or "Water the plants."
*   **How it works:** The patient gets a notification. They open it and tap **Done**. The task is finished.

### Guided Reminders
These are for tasks that take multiple steps. 
*   **Example:** "Morning Routine" or "Prepare Lunch."
*   **How it works:** The caregiver links the reminder to a Checklist. When the patient gets the notification, they tap **Start Now**, which opens the step-by-step checklist guide.

---

## 2. Setting Reminder Priority

Caregivers can assign a priority to a reminder:
*   **Normal:** Everyday tasks (e.g., watering plants).
*   **High:** Important tasks (e.g., eating lunch).
*   **Critical:** Must-do tasks (e.g., taking heart medication).

**Why Priority Matters:** 
If a patient skips a "Normal" reminder, it is just recorded normally. But if they skip a "High" or "Critical" reminder, the app flags this as an **Escalation Alert** in the caregiver's progress view. This helps the caregiver know immediately if something vital was missed.

---

## 3. Patient Actions on Reminders

When a reminder time arrives, the patient can choose:

*   **Done:** The task is completed. This adds to their daily progress streak.
*   **Snooze:** The patient is busy. The app will pause and remind them again in a few minutes.
*   **Skip:** The patient cannot or will not do the task. This is logged so the caregiver knows it was missed.

**What happens if they do nothing?**
If a reminder notification fires on the phone and the patient ignores it completely, it is eventually recorded as a "Missed" reminder. It will show up in the progress charts as incomplete.

---

## 4. How Caregivers Should Manage the Schedule

*   **Be clear and concise:** Use short titles. "Take blue pill" is better than "It's time to take your Lisinopril for your blood pressure."
*   **Don't overlap:** Try not to schedule three reminders for 9:00 AM. Space them out so the phone isn't buzzing constantly.
*   **Use repeats:** Set everyday tasks to repeat automatically (e.g., "Every Mon, Tue, Wed") so you don't have to recreate them manually.

## 5. A Note on Notifications
The app schedules reminders directly on the patient's phone. This means that even if the patient's phone temporarily loses internet connection, the reminder alarms will still go off at the right time. However, if the caregiver creates a *new* reminder, the patient's phone will need internet access to download the new schedule.
