# 02 — Caregiver Guide

As a caregiver, your role in this app is to gently guide and manage the daily digital environment for your loved one (the patient). The app gives you tools to organize their day while keeping their screen simple and stress-free.

---

## Your Role vs. The Patient's Role

*   **The Patient sees:** Only what they need to do *right now*. Big buttons, simple tasks, and supportive messages.
*   **You see:** A dashboard. You can view upcoming schedules, build routines, manage their contacts, and check their progress.

Because you have different roles, you and the patient can use the app on separate phones, even if you don't live in the same house.

## Getting Connected (The CareLink)

Before you can help manage the patient's schedule, you must link your accounts.

1.  Open your **Patients** tab.
2.  Tap to **Generate an Invite Code**.
3.  Give this short code to the patient.
4.  The patient opens their app, goes to their profile, and enters the code.
5.  Once accepted, you will have an "Active" link, giving you permission to manage their reminders and view their progress.

---

## Best Practices for Supporting the Patient

The app gives you many tools, but it is important to use them thoughtfully. People with early-stage Alzheimer's easily feel overwhelmed by too many tasks or too much information.

### 1. Start Small
When you first connect, do not fill their entire day with reminders. Start with **one or two simple reminders** (e.g., "Take morning pill" or "Drink a glass of water"). Let them get used to hearing the notification and tapping "Done."

### 2. Use Supportive Language
When you type a title for a reminder or a checklist, remember the patient will read it. 
*   **Instead of:** "Urgent: Take Heart Meds NOW"
*   **Try:** "Time for your morning medication"

### 3. Checklists are for Complex Routines
If a task requires multiple steps (like making coffee or getting dressed), don't create 5 different reminders. Instead, create one **Checklist**. The patient can then take their time going through it step-by-step.

### 4. Use Photos Where Possible
If you are testing the app in an environment that supports media uploads, use photos of actual items in the patient's house. A picture of *their* actual blue coffee mug is much more helpful than a generic icon.

### 5. Monitor, Don't Micromanage
Use the **Progress** tab to see if the patient is consistently missing reminders or abandoning checklists. If they are skipping a specific routine, it might mean the routine is too complicated, or scheduled at the wrong time. Use the data to adjust the schedule to fit their natural rhythm.

---

## Key Caregiver Actions

From your dashboard, you will primarily:

*   **Manage Reminders:** Add new tasks and set whether they repeat daily or weekly. You can assign a "Priority" to alert you if they skip something critical.
*   **Manage Checklists:** Build step-by-step guides.
*   **Review Progress:** Look at weekly summaries to see their "Streaks" (how many days in a row they have completed tasks). 
*   **Annotate Contacts:** Add helpful notes to the patient's contacts, such as "Your niece, lives in Cairo," to help them remember who people are.

## A Note on the App's Reality
Please remember that the app does not force the patient to do anything. If they put their phone in a drawer, the reminders will go unseen. Furthermore, in the current version, the patient's phone needs an active internet connection to tell your dashboard that a task was completed. The app works best when it is introduced gently as a helpful companion.
