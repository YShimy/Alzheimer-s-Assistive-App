# 07 — Feature Guide: Games and Progress

The app includes tools to track engagement and provide light cognitive stimulation. It is vital to understand how to interpret this data.

---

## 1. The Games Feature

The app includes two simple games in the current prototype:
1.  **Safe Path Memory:** A spatial game where the patient watches a path light up on a grid and tries to recreate it.
2.  **Routine Sequence:** A logic game where the patient arranges daily steps into the correct order.

### Purpose of the Games
These games are for **light mental stimulation and engagement**. They are designed to be low-pressure. 

**Important:** These games are *not* diagnostic tools. A lower score does not mean the patient's clinical condition is worsening, and a higher score is not a medical improvement. They simply offer a structured, safe way to interact with the device.

---

## 2. Understanding Progress

Both the patient and the caregiver have a "Progress" tab, but they look slightly different.

### What the Patient Sees
The patient sees a supportive, positive screen. They see their **Streaks**—how many consecutive days they have completed a reminder, finished a checklist, or played a game. 

The goal here is encouragement. If a streak breaks, the app doesn't scold them; it just invites them to start a new one.

### What the Caregiver Sees
The caregiver sees a more detailed dashboard. You will see:
*   **Adherence Rates:** Are they completing their reminders?
*   **Completion Rates:** Are they starting checklists but abandoning them halfway?
*   **Escalation Alerts:** If a reminder was marked "Critical" (like heart medication) and the patient skipped it, it flags here so you can follow up.

---

## 3. How to Use Progress Data Constructively

If you notice the patient's completion rates are dropping, **do not use the data to confront them.** 

Instead, use the data to adjust the app environment:
*   *Are they missing morning reminders?* Maybe the reminders are scheduled too early.
*   *Are they abandoning checklists?* The checklist might be too long. Break it into two smaller routines.
*   *Are they snoozing constantly?* The tasks might feel overwhelming. Try temporarily removing some non-essential reminders.

The Progress screen is a tool to help *you* build a better support system, not a report card for the patient.
