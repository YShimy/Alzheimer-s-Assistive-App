# 06 — Feature Guide: Contacts and Memory Aids

The Contacts feature in this app is not just a phone book. It is designed specifically as a **memory support tool** to help patients recognize and remember the important people in their lives.

---

## 1. What the Contacts Feature Does

For people with early-stage Alzheimer's, remembering faces, names, and relationships can become stressful. The Contacts screen provides a safe, curated list of familiar faces.

It displays:
*   The person's name.
*   A clear, recognizable photo (if added).
*   A "Relation" label (e.g., "Daughter", "Doctor").
*   A short "Memo" or helpful note.

## 2. How the Caregiver Helps

Patients can view their contacts, but the caregiver plays a vital role in organizing this list. 

From the caregiver dashboard, you can edit the patient's contacts to add memory cues.

### Using Memos Effectively
The "Memo" field is your chance to provide gentle context. 
*   *Poor memo:* "Works in accounting." (Not helpful for emotional recall).
*   *Good memo:* "Your youngest son. He visits every Sunday." (Provides warm, grounding context).

### Setting Priority Contacts
You can mark certain people as **"Priority"** or "Favorite" contacts. These people will appear at the top of the patient's list. Use this for the primary caregiver, a spouse, or an emergency contact, so the patient never has to scroll to find them.

---

## 3. The Power of Photos

If the current version of the app supports photo uploads, strongly encourage adding a profile picture for every contact. 

*   Use a recent photo that looks like the person *today*, not a photo from 20 years ago.
*   Ensure the person's face is clearly visible.
*   This visual confirmation heavily reduces the anxiety of trying to place a name.

---

## 4. Privacy and Respect

Remember that the Contacts list is personal. Only add people who are relevant and safe for the patient to contact. Do not fill the list with 50 acquaintances; keep it curated to the inner circle of family, close friends, and essential medical professionals to prevent confusion.
