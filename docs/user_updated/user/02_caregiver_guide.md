# 02 — Caregiver Guide

This guide explains how a caregiver is expected to use the app in the current version.

The app is meant to help you create a calmer and more structured support environment for the patient. It is not meant to replace personal care or judgment.

---

## What the Caregiver Role Is For

In the app, the caregiver role is mainly responsible for:

- helping organize reminders
- creating or refining checklists for multi-step tasks
- reviewing progress patterns
- helping the patient use the app without overload
- supporting memory-related content where that feature is available in the current version

The caregiver experience is intended to be more management-oriented than the patient experience.

---

## Best Way to Start

When setting up the app for a patient, start small.

A good first setup is usually:

- one or two simple reminders
- one useful checklist for a routine that often causes difficulty
- one language setting that feels natural for the patient

Avoid filling the app with too many tasks on day one.

---

## Creating Reminders

Use reminders for tasks that need a time-based prompt.

Examples:

- drink water
- take medication
- make a phone call
- attend an appointment

In the current version, reminder behavior may still vary depending on the exact implementation state of the build you are testing. In general, reminders are intended to support simple actions directly and more complex tasks through linked checklist flows where available.

### Good reminder-writing habits

- keep the title short
- use familiar wording
- avoid long explanations inside the reminder title
- space reminders out instead of clustering many at the same time

---

## Creating Checklists

Use a checklist when the patient may need **step-by-step support**.

Examples:

- morning routine
- making coffee
- getting ready to go out
- preparing something in the kitchen

A checklist is often better than creating many small reminders for one multi-step task.

Where supported in the current version, checklist steps may also include photos or short media cues.

---

## Using Progress Constructively

The progress area is meant to help you notice patterns.

For example:

- Are reminders usually completed?
- Are some tasks often snoozed?
- Are checklists started but not finished?
- Are some routines easier than others?

Use this information to improve the support plan, not to pressure the patient.

If a task is often missed, possible reasons include:

- the reminder time is not ideal
- the checklist is too long
- the task wording is unclear
- the patient needs a simpler version first

---

## Contacts and Memory Aids

If contact or memory-aid features are available in the build you are testing, use them carefully and keep entries simple.

Helpful patterns include:

- photo + name
- short relation note
- one brief helpful memo

Avoid adding too much text.

---

## Current-Version Reality Note

This is still a research prototype. Some caregiver capabilities may still be evolving, especially in areas like:

- multi-patient management
- remote/public deployment behavior
- media availability depending on environment setup
- some advanced progress or alert behaviors

So this guide should be read as a guide to the current prototype direction, not a claim that every advanced caregiver workflow is already polished.
