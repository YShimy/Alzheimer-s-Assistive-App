# 05 — Feature Guide: Checklists

While a Reminder is great for telling someone *when* to do something, a **Checklist** is designed to show them *how* to do it.

Checklists break down complex routines into simple, manageable steps, reducing confusion and anxiety.

---

## 1. What is a Checklist?

A Checklist is a routine built by the caregiver. It consists of a Title (e.g., "Making Coffee") and an ordered list of steps. 

Instead of showing the patient a daunting list of 10 things to do all at once, the app shows them **one step at a time**. Once they finish step 1, they tap a button, and step 2 appears.

---

## 2. Examples of Great Checklists

Checklists are best used for routines that have a specific order, or tasks where the patient might forget what comes next.

**Example: Morning Preparation**
1. Put on your glasses.
2. Wash your face with warm water.
3. Put on your blue sweater.
4. Come to the kitchen for breakfast.

**Example: Making Tea**
1. Fill the kettle with water.
2. Turn the kettle on.
3. Put a teabag in the green mug.
4. Pour the hot water carefully.

---

## 3. Adding Media to Steps (If Supported)

If your current version of the app supports Media Uploads, this is one of the most powerful features you can use.

Caregivers can attach a **photo or short video** to a specific step.
*   *Text step:* "Take your heart medication."
*   *With Media:* The text is accompanied by a clear photo of the exact pill bottle sitting on the patient's actual kitchen counter.

Visual cues are incredibly powerful for memory support. Seeing a familiar object makes the task feel much easier and more concrete.

---

## 4. How Patients Use Checklists

Patients can start a checklist in two ways:
1.  **From a Reminder:** If a reminder is "Guided", tapping "Start Now" launches the checklist automatically.
2.  **From the Checklist Tab:** The patient can browse their available checklists and start one whenever they want.

Once started, the app records this as an "In Progress" run. If they get distracted and put the phone down, the app knows the checklist was "Abandoned." If they finish all the steps, it is marked as "Completed."

---

## 5. Caregiver Tips for Writing Checklists

*   **Keep steps bite-sized:** "Make breakfast" is too big. Break it down into getting the cereal, getting the milk, and getting a spoon.
*   **Use hints:** The app allows you to add a "Hint" to a step. Use this for gentle clarification (e.g., Step: "Lock the front door." Hint: "Turn the key to the right.").
*   **Review abandoned routines:** If you notice in the Progress tab that a patient keeps abandoning the "Making Coffee" checklist halfway through, the routine might be too complicated. Try editing the checklist to have fewer steps or clearer photos.
